# ProofLink Product Recommendations

## Core recommendation

Do not position ProofLink as a generic retirement-plan AI tool.

Position it as a deterministic payroll-to-recordkeeper control layer that finds operational errors before they become audit, correction, participant, or fiduciary problems.

The strongest commercial wedge is:

> Payroll compensation integrity and match-impact detection.

## Why this wedge is stronger than generic reconciliation

Basic reconciliation is useful, but it can be dismissed as internal operations cleanup.

Compensation-definition and match-impact errors are more valuable because they connect directly to:

- participant harm
- employer correction dollars
- audit findings
- plan document compliance
- payroll/vendor accountability
- advisor differentiation

A buyer pays faster when the product can say:

> "Here are the employees affected, here is the estimated dollar exposure, here is the likely root cause, and here is the evidence packet."

## Buyer priority

### First buyer: retirement plan advisors / consultants

They need differentiation and a reason to engage sponsors beyond investments, fees, and education.

Advisor pitch:

> "We run a payroll-to-recordkeeper integrity scan to identify contribution, match, eligibility, and compensation issues before they become audit or correction problems."

### Second buyer: sponsors with operational complexity

Best sponsor profiles:

- employer match
- hourly or part-time workforces
- multiple pay codes
- recent payroll or recordkeeper conversion
- audited plan
- safe harbor match or true-up complexity
- known payroll or HR turnover

### Lower-priority buyer

Do not start with sponsors who have no match, no audit pressure, and clean payroll data. They will not feel enough urgency.

## Features to avoid for now

Avoid these until there is buyer validation:

- plan document AI assistant
- broad compliance chatbot
- dashboard redesign
- CRM workflow
- API integrations
- automated correction engine
- billing/subscription logic
- multi-tenant enterprise SaaS hardening

Those can come later. Right now they add complexity without proving willingness to pay.

## Demo strategy

The demo should be built around a single painful story:

1. Payroll export includes regular pay, overtime, bonus, and excluded pay.
2. Plan rule says which compensation counts.
3. Actual match does not line up.
4. ProofLink identifies under-match/over-match cases.
5. ProofLink quantifies estimated dollars.
6. ProofLink generates an evidence pack.
7. Advisor uses the report to start a corrective conversation with sponsor, payroll, TPA, or recordkeeper.

## Commercial test

Before building beyond the auditor feature, ask five retirement advisors or TPAs this exact question:

> "If I gave you a report that identified compensation-definition and match errors from payroll and recordkeeper files, quantified likely correction dollars, and produced evidence you could hand to the sponsor or auditor, would you use it in plan reviews or prospecting?"

Follow-up question:

> "Would you pay for that per plan, per audit season, or as part of an advisor practice package?"

If they do not care about the dollar exposure or evidence packet, the wedge is weaker than expected.

## Recommended pricing hypothesis

Do not start with monthly SaaS pricing.

Test these pricing models first:

- per-plan diagnostic scan
- advisor practice package for a fixed number of plans
- audit-season batch review
- TPA/advisor white-label report

Initial pricing hypothesis:

- small plan diagnostic: low hundreds per scan
- audited/complex plan diagnostic: higher hundreds to low thousands per scan
- advisor practice package: monthly or annual after proven usage

This is only a hypothesis. Do not build billing until someone says yes to the diagnostic.

## Product principle

Every feature must support one of these outcomes:

1. Find an operational retirement-plan error.
2. Quantify the financial impact.
3. Identify who needs to fix it.
4. Produce evidence that survives audit review.

If a feature does not support one of those four outcomes, defer it.
